# Thermal state → hot clearance → sealing screen

Status: **screening only; no material or sealing architecture is promoted.**

This campaign addresses the missing quantity in the earlier 2/3/5 µm
annular brackets: the piston and liner temperatures are not known to be equal.
It is a small, inspectable thermal RC network, not pseudo-FEA.  The chain is

```
p(theta), T_gas(theta), piston speed(theta)
    -> solid-node temperatures over repeated cycles
    -> signed hot radial clearance
    -> positive-clearance annulus sensitivity only
```

## What was run

The default history is generated by `microengine_rig.py` using its existing
constant wall closure (`h = 600 W/(m² K)`, 2 g, 850 J/(kg K)).  It is a
calculated proxy, not a measured heat-flux history and not CFD.  At 1200 rpm a
360° modeled pass is 50 ms; a 50 ms idle/cooling segment makes the modeled
four-stroke period 100 ms.  The pass and idle segment are extrapolations of the
available one-revolution history.  The proxy peaks at 77.18 bar and 1100 K.

Seven nodes are integrated with explicit `m c_p dT/dt`: piston crown, piston
skirt, rod/crank, liner TDC band, lower liner, head/deck, and block/ambient.
Solid links are bidirectional conductance brackets.  Piston and liner links
scale linearly with the selected material conductivity relative to the
Al-4032/4140 reference pair; this is a transparent `kA/L` screen, not a CAD
or finite-element extraction.  The default node capacities and link values
are recorded in `data/thermal/thermal_state_summary.json`.

Two gas-side closures are deliberately separated:

* `constant_h`: the existing repository closure, 600 W/(m² K).
* `angle_correlation`: a clipped Woschni-shaped pressure/temperature/piston-
  speed sensitivity used only as an engineering sensitivity case.  It is not
  a validated miniature-engine correlation.

Material CTE/thermal-conductivity inputs are preserved in
[`data/materials/thermal_properties.json`](data/materials/thermal_properties.json)
with source, range and uncertainty notes.  The model keeps negative hot
clearance as contact/interference; it never converts it to an annular flow.

## Primary 8.5 mm result

For the representative EN AW-4032 piston / AISI 4140 liner at 293.15 K
reference, the final-cycle RC envelope is:

| gas-side case | piston skirt (K) | liner TDC band (K) | cold fit for 2–5 µm hot (µm radial) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| constant `h=600` | 328.11–328.42 | 333.30–334.43 | **2.82–5.78** |
| angle sensitivity | 349.20–349.71 | 351.38–353.04 | **3.63–6.57** |

These temperatures are low because the only available gas-side input is the
existing lumped 600 W/(m² K) proxy and the node masses/conductances are
engineering brackets.  They should not be read as a prediction of the actual
engine thermal state.  The important result is the sensitivity to the
unmeasured piston–liner temperature difference: a nominal **3 µm cold** fit
would give only 2.18–2.22 µm hot under the constant-`h` proxy, but 1.37–1.42 µm
under the angle sensitivity.  The latter is below the desired 2 µm lower edge.

The broader bounded sensitivity grid (54 cases around Al-4032/4140, with
`h`×0.75/1/1.25, cooling ×0.5/1/1.5, and gas-history offsets ±20 K) gives:

* lower cold-fit bound for ≥2 µm hot: **2.66–3.97 µm** (5th–95th percentiles);
* upper cold-fit bound for ≤5 µm hot: **5.63–6.91 µm** (5th–95th percentiles).

Those are percentiles of an explicit engineering sensitivity grid, **not
confidence or production probabilities**.  The complete rows are in
`data/thermal/thermal_state_uncertainty.csv`.

## Material screen

The eight screened pairs are Al-4032/4140, Al-2618/4140, Al-4032/gray iron,
Al-2618/gray iron, Al-4032/6061, steel/steel, silicon-nitride/4140, and
silicon-nitride/gray iron.  The calculated fit window is a thermal-fit result,
not a recommendation: aluminum pistons against steel/iron liners require
more cold radial clearance as piston temperature rises; matched steel is
less differential-expansion-sensitive; silicon nitride is low-CTE but has
conductivity, brittleness, surface-finish, lubrication and manufacturability
questions outside this scalar model; an aluminum liner changes wear/galling
and tribology questions rather than solving them.

The earlier analytical inversion remains the cleanest temperature sensitivity
reference.  For Al-4032/4140 at 8.5 mm, the cold radial clearance required for
**3 µm hot** is approximately 10.66 µm at 450/400 K, 12.09 µm at 500/450 K,
and 16.49 µm at 550/450 K (piston/liner).  A ±25 or ±50 K uncertainty can
therefore move the required fit by several micrometres.  At 12.5 mm the same
3 µm-hot states require approximately 14.27, 16.38 and 22.84 µm: the absolute
differential-expansion penalty grows with bore.  The larger mule is valuable
for metrology and instrumentation, not because it removes CTE mismatch.

## Leakage interpretation

Only positive hot clearance is passed to `physics/annulus.py`; pressure,
temperature, viscosity, bore, skirt length and eccentricity remain explicit.
Cold static leak-down and hot dynamic in-cylinder blow-by remain separate
lanes.  The annulus output is an uncalibrated sensitivity, not a predicted
hardware flow and is not added to `data/leakage/records.csv`.

The cubic clearance sensitivity is consequential: at 8.5 mm, 45 bar upstream,
1 bar downstream and 1100 K air, the present annulus screen gives about
3.21 mg/s at 2.51 µm hot versus 56.5 mg/s at 6.53 µm hot (zero/interference
returns `contact_invalid_annulus`).  This illustrates why a temperature error
cannot be hidden inside one leak-down percentage.

## Startup trap and design questions

The inverse fit is an intersection over the complete warm-up cycle, not only a
steady endpoint.  A cold fit selected to survive the hottest state can leak
more when the parts are cold; a fit that is tight at room temperature can pass
through zero clearance during warm-up.  The current model does not calculate
contact pressure, oil-film failure, taper, piston rock, or scuffing, so it
cannot choose between ringless ABC-style fit and a ring.

The defensible answer tonight is therefore:

> For the current Al-4032/4140 screen, individual proxy cases require roughly
> **2.7–6.9 µm cold radial clearance** for a 2–5 µm hot target.  The overlap
> that remains conservative across the reported percentile envelope is about
> **4.0–5.6 µm**, pending measured temperatures.  The controlling uncertainty
> is the actual piston–liner temperature difference; the frequently quoted
> “3 µm” is not a complete fit specification.

This does not establish that the ringless architecture is feasible.  It says
the 8.5 mm engine is not ruled out by first-order thermal expansion, provided
the temperature field and warm fit are measured and controlled.

## Fixture connection (Issue #13)

The next experiment should be a warm direct-flow fixture on a 10–15 mm reference
cylinder.  At every point measure cold bore diameter/roundness and axial taper,
piston diameter at crown/skirt and thrust/anti-thrust locations, piston and
liner temperatures independently, chamber absolute pressure, ambient pressure,
gas temperature, direct mass or volumetric flow, and lubricant identity,
viscosity/condition and quantity.  Crankcase outlet flow is a useful separate
closure channel where available.

Use stabilized cold (293 K) and warm states, for example 350/325 K,
450/400 K and 550/450 K piston/liner pairs, at approximately 2, 4 and 6.5 bar
absolute for the initial static matrix; add 25/45 bar only with a rated fixture.
Take at least three repeats and record actual pressure/temperature, not only
regulator settings.  Size the sensor for roughly **0.1–100 mg/s** with about
0.1 mg/s low-end resolution as coverage guidance from the current model.  This
is sensor sizing, not an asserted leakage range.

## AP .09 / Hornet evidence

The literature search recovered qualitative near-scale geometry/test
references but no trustworthy machine-readable absolute airflow/blow-by row
with enough simultaneous pressure, temperature and fit metadata to enter the
measured leakage schema.  No synthetic or graph-digitized point was added to
`data/leakage/records.csv`.  SAE 2010-32-0018 remains a lead for a thesis/report
or author-supplied table; a future digitization must retain figure/page and
uncertainty provenance.

The principal public lead is [SAE 2010-32-0018](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/experimental-tests-simulations-a-15-cc-miniature-glow-ignition-two-stroke-engine-2010-32-0018).

## Reproduction

```bash
python scripts/thermal_state_rc.py --plots
python -m unittest tests/test_thermal_state.py tests/test_thermal_clearance.py -v
python -m py_compile physics/thermal_state.py scripts/thermal_state_rc.py
```

The generated history and compact summaries are deliberately versioned; large
deterministic sweep tables are not.  Replace the proxy history with a measured
or CFD-derived `deg,P_bar,T_K,pistonVelocity_m_s` CSV using `--history` when
the fixture or a wall-heat-flux run exists.
