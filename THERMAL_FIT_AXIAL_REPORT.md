# Axial thermal-fit feasibility screen (low idle)

Status: bounded screening study; not a manufacturing drawing, architecture
promotion, CFD result, or calibrated leakage prediction.

This study extends the existing seven-node thermal RC result to a small axial
profile so that corresponding crown/upper-piston/skirt and liner stations can
be checked with the signed radial clearance equation. The current repository
has no measured axial piston/liner temperature pair, taper map, roundness map,
or direct-flow result. Consequently, the axial gradients and shape amplitudes
below are explicit model envelopes only. They are not recovered ABC dimensions.

## Classification and retained topology

* **LITERATURE-DERIVED CALCULATION:** temperature-dependent cumulative strain
  profiles and conductivity values from `data/materials/thermal_properties.json`.
  Ringed literature (Tada/Furuhama heat paths and ring discharge coefficients)
  is recorded as ringed-only evidence and is not injected into the ringless
  baseline.
* **PROJECT MODEL RESULT:** existing `thermal_state.py` RC temperatures, then
  linear interpolation between its piston crown/skirt and liner TDC/lower
  nodes, signed radial hot clearance, and a series axial-annulus sensitivity.
  The head, block-to-ambient, rod/crank, and solid links remain explicit in the
  existing topology. `constant_h` remains the baseline at 600 W/(m2 K);
  `angle_correlation` is reported separately as a clipped Woschni-shaped
  sensitivity closure.
* **INFERENCE:** five corresponding axial stations, 8 mm axial bracket length,
  ±2 µm illustrative liner/piston taper or barrel amplitudes, ±1 µm radial
  machining-error offsets, and a 1 µm contact-margin screen. None is an ABC
  measurement or tolerance claim.
* **UNKNOWN:** local axial temperatures, piston rock/eccentricity and
  roundness, actual taper/pinch, contact pressure/elastic flattening, surface
  roughness, oil-film state, ringless heat-transfer coefficient, ring geometry,
  and calibrated flow.

All clearances in this report are **radial**. Bore and piston dimensions in the
underlying equation are diameters, so the one-half diameter conversion is kept
explicit. `c_hot <= 0` is contact/interference and is never treated as an
annulus flow area.

## Small axial model

For `x=z/L`, the local cold radial clearance is:

`c_cold(z) = c_base + A_liner(1-2x) - A_piston_taper(2x-1) - 4 A_barrel x(1-x) + e_machine`.

The signs are documented in `physics/thermal_fit_axial.py`: positive liner
taper opens the crown/TDC end, positive piston taper makes the skirt end
larger, and positive barrel makes the piston largest mid-length. These terms
are intentionally generic screening knobs. The local piston and liner
temperatures are linearly interpolated from the resolved RC nodes and then fed
to the existing `calculate_clearance` implementation; no conductance is
invented for an axial gradient.

For a positive-clearance path only, station restrictions are combined using
the transparent laminar-annulus sensitivity `1/c_eq^3 = mean(1/c_i^3)`.
This represents series axial resistance and is not a measured flow law. If any
station is zero or negative, the result is `contact_invalid_annulus` with no
mass-flow number.

## Results from the supported 1200 rpm proxy

The source history is `data/thermal/engine_history_proxy.csv`, generated by the
existing nonreacting `microengine_rig.py` proxy. It is not measured and does
not supply a wall heat-flux correlation. The existing 50 ms idle segment is
retained only at 1200 rpm (one revolution); no 800–3000 rpm claim is made. A
speed sweep would need a speed-consistent history and idle duration (`60/N`)
before its thermal timing can be interpreted.

For the representative Al-4032 piston / 4140 liner, neutral shape and zero
machining error give:

| bore | closure | RC warm-up min path, 3 µm cold (µm) | RC periodic min path, 3 µm cold (µm) | neutral axial base fit for 2–5 µm hot (µm radial) |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8.5 mm | constant `h=600` | **1.32** | **−3.92 contact** | **8.90–11.71** |
| 8.5 mm | angle sensitivity | **−0.47 contact** | **−7.80 contact** | **12.76–15.40** |
| 12.5 mm bridge | constant `h=600` | **−2.77 contact** | **−11.87 contact** | **16.82–19.28** |
| 12.5 mm bridge | angle sensitivity | **−7.67 contact** | **−18.64 contact** | **23.52–25.67** |

The 8.5 mm constant-h neutral periodic profile at the worst sampled pass is:

| station | piston K | liner K | hot radial gap at 10 µm cold (µm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| crown | 440.16 | 398.23 | 3.10 |
| upper piston | 439.00 | 397.19 | 3.15 |
| ringland / mid-skirt | 437.85 | 396.15 | 3.19 |
| lower skirt | 436.69 | 395.12 | 3.23 |
| skirt end | 435.54 | 394.08 | 3.27 |

This is a bounded interpolation result, not evidence that the actual piston
has a linear axial temperature field. The crown is the limiting station in
this proxy. The larger bridge amplifies differential expansion; it is useful
for metrology and instrumentation, not a proof of 8.5 mm scaling.

## Taper, barrel, machining-error and leakage sensitivity

The candidate screen evaluates 8 shape cases × 3 signed machining offsets for
each bore and closure (96 compact rows). For 8.5 mm, the full illustrative
shape/error envelope shifts the required base fit to:

| closure | lower-bound range (µm) | upper-bound range (µm) | interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| constant h | 7.90–11.90 | 8.71–12.71 | several ±2 µm shape cases have no common 2–5 µm intersection |
| angle sensitivity | 11.76–15.76 | 12.40–16.40 | angle closure raises the fit requirement and causes contact at 10 µm in most cases |

The extrema from ±1 µm machining error move the base-fit bounds by roughly
±1 µm. A neutral 10 µm cold fit has a positive 8.5 mm constant-h path in this
proxy (`c_eq ≈ 3.18 µm` at the sampled worst profile), but a shaped candidate
can narrow the path materially. Among the 21 shape/error rows evaluated at a
10 µm cold base, the angle sensitivity has contact in 19; those rows
deliberately report no annulus flow. The 12.5 mm bridge at a 10 µm base is
contact for the neutral and all 21 shape/error rows in the periodic proxy.

Leakage pressure and gas temperature are paired to the same periodic history
row as the worst axial clearance. For the neutral 8.5 mm constant-h 10 µm
base, that row is approximately +60 CAD, 18.589 bar and 721.66 K, giving
`c_eq ≈ 3.18 µm` and **1.866 mg/s** in the uncalibrated positive-annulus
sensitivity. A BDC pressure/temperature pair would give about 0.111 mg/s for
the same gap and is not used as the worst-state result.

The additional `cold_tdc_pinch_demo` intentionally uses a 1 µm base with a
−2 µm top pinch to exercise the zero/negative cold-clearance branch (including
the ±1 µm error cases). It is a mathematical contact example, not a proposed
room-temperature fit.

These outcomes answer feasibility only in the limited first-order sense: a
positive, nonuniform path can exist in some 8.5 mm constant-h cases, while a
few-micrometre shape or temperature error can remove the margin. No contact
pressure or seizure load is calculated. “Below 1 µm margin” is a conservative
screening flag, not a seizure prediction.

### Conditional preheat check

`minimum_preheat_temperature_K` is included to expose the missing local
temperature split. It now scans the bounded temperature interval and reports
whether the safe region is `minimum_safe`, `maximum_safe`, `always`, `never`,
or a bounded interval; common preheat is not assumed to help monotonically.
For the 8.5 mm constant-h neutral lower-bound fit, an assumed liner 30 K hotter
than the piston is `maximum_safe` over the 293.15–700 K scan: the conditional
1 µm margin is met at **Tp/Tl = 293.15/323.15 K** and persists only to about
**621.17/651.17 K**. The angle-sensitivity and both 12.5 mm neutral cases are
`always` over that bounded scan (their reported threshold pair is likewise
293.15/323.15 K). These are CTE-only conditional intervals, not a safe-crank
permission. Preheat does not cure the periodic 3 µm contact result or identify
the real required temperature; paired station measurements and lubrication /
contact evidence are still required.

### Ringless and ringed paths

The leakage rows are **ringless positive-clearance axial-annulus** sensitivities
only. Ringed heat-path fractions and 0.8–0.9 discharge-coefficient literature
remain separate ringed evidence; no ring coefficient is applied to this
ringless thermal network or used to claim ringed leakage. A ringed comparison
requires ring geometry, land temperatures and direct pressure/flow data.

At low speed, mixed/boundary lubrication risk is an open tribology question:
the model does not calculate film thickness, roughness ratio, load, oil
viscosity under DME exposure, or friction. It does not claim that no gas or
oil film exists. The correct next screen is a measured warm-flow and
lubrication-state matrix, not a Hersey-number or “no film” assertion.

## Simulation → weakness → minimal fix

1. The existing RC simulation supplies distinct crown/skirt and liner-band
   temperatures and exposes warm-up versus periodic contact.
2. Axial interpolation exposes that the result is controlled by an unmeasured
   local piston–liner temperature difference and by a single narrow station;
   shape/error envelopes can erase the nominal fit intersection.
3. The minimal justified fix is the corresponding-station warm fixture in
   Issue #13: measure piston diameter, bore/roundness and axial taper, paired
   piston/liner temperatures, pressure/gas state, lubricant condition, and
   direct flow at at least three axial stations and repeated cold/warm states.
   Replace the interpolation and shape envelopes with those observations;
   retain ringless and ringed reductions as separate lanes.
